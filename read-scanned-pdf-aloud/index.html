How to read a scanned PDF aloud

An old journal can look perfectly legible on your phone, yet pressing a word selects the whole page. The PDF may hold a photograph of the print. Neural Reader can recognize the words in that image and let you check them before listening.

Try one representative page

  1. Find out what kind of page you have

    Try selecting one sentence in your PDF viewer. Selectable words usually indicate a text layer. If the words sit inside a single image, try a scan. A document can contain both kinds, so check a page from the chapter itself as well as the cover.

  2. Open that page in Neural Reader

    Tap +, choose Read aloud, then File. Pick the PDF, choose Range, and use the same start and end number for a one-page test. Count from the first page of the file. A printed page marked “1” might follow several pages of front matter.

  3. Follow the passage before tapping Read

    Compare the first sentence in Extracted text with the page. Check the jump between columns, the next heading, and the closing line. You can move a misplaced passage or correct a word in the app, then tap Read.

What if only some pages come through?

A searchable introduction can sit in front of a chapter made entirely of scans. If the introduction comes through and the chapter is missing, select a range containing only the scanned chapter. Neural Reader can recognize up to 20 scanned PDF pages per request. For a longer chapter, start another range at the next page.

Optical character recognition (OCR) turns printed shapes into editable words. Give it a page of body text for your first test. A decorative title page or a chart has little continuous prose to hear. If a scan is too faint to read when enlarged, look for a sharper copy or an original digital edition.

Make captions, notes, and numbers sound right

A side caption can cut a spoken sentence in half. Move the caption to the end of its paragraph if you want the context, or remove it from your listening copy. For two columns, read the final line of the first and the opening line of the second together.

For a lab handout, compare “0.5 mL” with the original before listening. A lost decimal changes the quantity. You can write “half a milliliter” in Extracted text when that is what the author meant. An editor’s eye is especially valuable for abbreviations, formulas, and names.

Carry the last sentence into the next page

Read the words on either side of a page break together. Keep the space in “the lighthouse”, join a word divided only by the printed line ending, and check for a running title in between. Once the test passage sounds right, open the rest of your range and settle into the chapter.
